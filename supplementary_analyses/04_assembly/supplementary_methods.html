Supplementary Methods

Supplementary single-cell, copy-number-adjusted co-expression and Mendelian randomization analyses

1. Single-cell datasets and curation

Six public cervical single-cell RNA-seq datasets with raw or recoverable counts were used (Additional file 1: Table S10): GSE208653 (4 normal, 2 HSIL, 3 cancer), E-MTAB-11948 (3 squamous carcinomas with matched adjacent normal tissue), GSE197461 (8 carcinomas: 3 squamous, 5 adenocarcinoma), GSE236738 (3 treatment-naive tumors only), GSE173231 (10 normal ectocervical biopsies) and GSE333892 (23 carcinomas, 10 normal, 19 squamous intraepithelial lesions [SIL]). GSE168652 was excluded because its libraries duplicate E-MTAB-11948 patient 3 (barcode Jaccard index 0.994 and 0.996; pseudobulk Pearson r=0.966 and 0.976); GSE197461 and GSE208653 showed no barcode overlap and were both retained. GSE171894 was excluded because only normalized values are available.

Two file repairs were required. In E-MTAB-11948 the deposited sample-5 feature file was identical (md5) to its barcode file, so the feature file of sample 1 was used after confirming that the feature files of samples 1–4 and 6 were identical and matched the matrix dimension (36,601 genes). The GSE333892 Seurat object stores log-normalized values in both its counts and data layers; integer counts were recovered as expm1(data) × nCount_RNA / 10,000. In 3,000 randomly selected cells, all non-zero recovered values were within 0.01 of an integer and recovered library sizes equalled nCount_RNA.

2. Quality control and annotation

Each sample was processed separately in Scanpy 1.11.5 (Python 3.13) with the thresholds of the original analysis: 300–7,499 detected genes, <20% mitochondrial UMIs, doublet removal with the Scrublet implementation in Scanpy (fixed threshold 0.25), and removal of genes detected in <3 cells. Cell types were assigned by Leiden clustering (2,000 highly variable genes, 30 principal components, 15 neighbors, resolution 1.0) followed by cluster-level scoring of the canonical marker sets used in the original analysis. In total, 561,330 cells from 88 samples passed QC. GSE173231 contained only 723 epithelial cells (fibroblast-depleted, CD8-enriched protocol) and was not used as a normal-epithelium reference.

3. Copy-number inference and malignant-cell calls

Copy number was inferred per sample with infercnvpy 0.6.1 (window 250 genes, step 10) using the same sample's immune and stromal cells (T/NK, B, plasma, myeloid, mast, endothelial, fibroblast, smooth muscle) as reference, with GRCh38 gene positions and UCSC hg38 cytobands. For each cell, a CNV score (mean squared CNV value) and a CNV correlation (Pearson correlation with the mean profile of the 5% highest-scoring epithelial cells) were computed. Epithelial cells were called malignant when both metrics exceeded the 95th percentile of the sample's reference cells, non-malignant when both were at or below it, and unresolved otherwise; a 99th-percentile rule was used as a sensitivity analysis. A cell was called 19p13.3-gained when the mean gene-level CNV of 19p13.3 genes exceeded the 99th percentile of reference cells.

As a cross-check, CopyKAT 1.2.5 (R 4.6.1) was run on up to 1,500 epithelial and 500 reference cells per sample (ngene.chr=5, win.size=25, KS.cut=0.1, reference cells supplied as normal). Because CopyKAT labelled all cells diploid in this mode despite clonal changes in its own CNA matrix, the same 95th-percentile rule was applied to the CopyKAT CNA matrix. Agreement was summarized by percentage agreement and Cohen's κ among epithelial cells called definitively by both methods.

4. Pseudobulk LONP1 comparisons

LONP1 was summarized per sample and cell category as pseudobulk log2(CPM+1) from summed raw counts; units with <100 cells were excluded (30 cells for the 19p13.3 gain comparison). The normal-epithelium reference comprised non-malignant epithelium from the 4 GSE208653 normal and 2 E-MTAB-11948 adjacent normal samples, extended in the pooled analysis by the 8 GSE333892 normal samples with sufficient epithelium (14 normal units). Malignant and normal epithelium were compared by ordinary least squares with dataset as a fixed effect, supported by Mann-Whitney tests and cell-level linear mixed models (random intercept per sample; statsmodels). Within-tumor comparisons used paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and cell-level mixed models. In GSE333892, normal, SIL and malignant epithelium were compared by Kruskal-Wallis and pairwise Mann-Whitney tests. Because GEO provides no SIL grade, grade was inferred from sample identifiers (5 HSIL/CIN3-labelled, 1 ASCUS, 13 not inferable), and SIL samples with ≥20% CNV-positive epithelium were classed as CNV-high.

5. Copy-number-adjusted co-expression

TCGA-CESC PanCancer Atlas expression (RSEM), continuous log2 copy number and arm-level data were obtained from cBioPortal (cesc_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018). The original genome-wide Spearman co-expression was reproduced in 294 tumors (correlation with the published coefficients r=0.9986). Partial Spearman correlations were computed in the 290 tumors with copy-number data by rank-transforming all variables, regressing the ranks of LONP1 and of each gene on the covariate ranks, and correlating the residuals. Model A adjusted for LONP1 and partner-gene copy number; model B additionally adjusted for 19p arm-level copy number (mean log2 ratio of 19p genes) and ABSOLUTE tumor purity (n=240). Enrichment of 19p13.3 genes was tested by two-sided Fisher's exact test at rho≥0.4 and for the top 164 genes. Pre-ranked enrichment used fgsea 1.38.0 (fgseaMultilevel; gene sets of 15–500 genes; GO:BP and Reactome from msigdbr) on the raw and partial coefficients.

6. Mendelian randomization

Exposures. Genetically predicted expression of LONP1 and 15 prespecified partner genes: the ten strongest copy-number-adjusted co-expression partners outside chromosome 19 (STUB1, C21orf70, PHB1, PMPCA, MRPS2, EXOSC4, MRPL12, NPRL3, DHODH and MIF) and the mitochondrial unfolded protein response module (TRAP1, PHB2, HSPD1, HSPE1 and ATF4). Chromosome 19 was excluded to avoid the 19p amplicon. Instruments were cis-eQTLs (±1 Mb) from eQTLGen whole blood (2019-12-11 release) and GTEx v8 blood, uterus, vagina and cultured fibroblasts from the eQTL Catalogue. eQTLGen Z-scores were converted to per-SD effects as β=Z/√[2f(1−f)(N+Z²)] using 1000 Genomes EUR allele frequencies.

Outcomes. FinnGen R12 cervical cancer (878 cases, 222,078 controls; discovery), carcinoma in situ (3,701 cases), squamous cell carcinoma (222) and adenocarcinoma (142); Pan-UKB European-ancestry ICD-10 C53 (273), phecode 180.1 (299) and cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, phecode 180.3 (2,186); and BioBank Japan cervical cancer (967 cases, 60,614 controls; replication). All data were harmonized on GRCh37 (GRCh38 sources lifted over with the UCSC chain) and aligned to 1000 Genomes phase 3 alleles; palindromic variants with allele frequency 0.42–0.58 were removed.

Instruments and estimation. Instruments had P<5×10−8, were clumped in PLINK 1.90b7.2 (r²<0.1, 1,000 kb; 1000 Genomes EUR, or EAS for BBJ) and had F>10. One instrument was analyzed by the Wald ratio; several by inverse-variance weighting (fixed effects, or multiplicative random effects with >3 instruments and Cochran's Q P<0.05), with MR-Egger and weighted-median estimates when ≥3 instruments were available. Direction was checked by Steiger filtering. Estimates are odds ratios per 1-SD increase in predicted expression; the Benjamini-Hochberg FDR was applied across genes within each exposure source and outcome. Estimates were obtained with MendelianRandomization 0.10.0 (R 4.6.1) and reproduced by an independent Python implementation (maximum absolute difference in IVW/Wald β 4.4×10−16 across 182 estimates; identical significance calls).

Colocalization. coloc.abf (coloc 5.2.3) was applied across the ±1 Mb region for each gene-outcome pair using the four GTEx tissues and the complete eQTLGen cis-eQTL file, with priors p1=p2=10−4 and p12=10−5 (sensitivity p12=5×10−6); PP.H4>0.8 was considered strong evidence of a shared causal variant. Colocalization was repeated for the 24 genes within ±1 Mb of LONP1 with a GTEx blood cis-eQTL (P<10−4). Reporting follows STROBE-MR (Additional file 1: Table S11); full results are in Additional file 1: Table S12.
